  1. Honey bee injection (adults, queens and pupae)

    A general protocol for injecting virus or a control solution (PBS) into newly emerged adult workers, queens and white-eyed pupae. Decide the number of colonies, individuals per colony and treatment groups for your own experimental design. Within each colony, assign individuals randomly to the virus and control groups.

    Materials

    • Bee housing cups (20 oz clear plastic cups with snap-on lids)
    • 1.5 mL tubes (perforated for sugar syrup, half-cut for pollen patty)
    • 15 mL tubes for sample collection and storage
    • Virus inoculum and PBS (control)
    • Ethanol, Kimwipes, gloves, labels and markers
    • Petri dishes and filter paper (70–90 mm)
    • Soft flat forceps and wax sheets
    • Sugar syrup and pollen patties
    • Ice or CO₂ for anesthesia
    • Microinjector (UMP3 or equivalent) with camera display
    • Hamilton syringe (25 µL); needles with 12° and 20° bevel
    • Incubator (34 °C, 50% relative humidity)

    Before injection day

    • Calibrate the microinjector.
    • Prepare the housing cups, feeding tubes and wax sheets.
    • Label cups and Petri dishes by colony and treatment.
    • Set the incubator to 34 °C and 50% relative humidity.
    • Practise injections on a few adult bees and pupae.
    • For adult injections: the evening before, collect brood frames with bees expected to emerge overnight and keep them in the incubator.

    Adult bee injection

    1. Collect newly emerged adult bees from the incubated brood frames directly into labeled housing cups.
    2. Randomly assign bees from each colony to the virus or PBS group.
    3. Anesthetize the bees on ice or with CO₂.
    4. Inject 2 µL between the 1st–2nd or 2nd–3rd abdominal segments using the microinjector with a 12° needle.
    5. Return the bees to their labeled cups and give sugar syrup and pollen patty through the modified 1.5 mL tubes.
    6. Keep the cups in the incubator at 34 °C and 50% RH.
    7. Remove dead bees right after injection and throughout the experiment.
    8. At 72 hours post-injection, collect the surviving bees into 15 mL tubes and store at −80 °C.

    Queen injection

    1. Inject the queen in the thorax.
    2. Put her in a cup with 10 worker bees to take care of her.
    Important: queens received from another source come with their own attendant workers. Use those same workers with the queen after injection. Workers from a different colony may attack and kill her.

    Pupal injection

    1. Collect brood frames with white-eyed pupae.
    2. Gently remove pupae from the cells with soft forceps, without damaging them.
    3. Place them in labeled Petri dishes lined with filter paper (about 10 pupae per dish), by colony and treatment.
    4. Keep the dishes in the incubator (34 °C, 50% RH) for at least 2 hours before injection. Exclude any pupae that turn dark (melanization), as this suggests injury and immune activation.
    5. Randomly assign pupae from each colony to the virus or PBS group.
    6. Inject 2 µL between the 1st–2nd or 2nd–3rd abdominal segments with a 20° needle.
    7. Return pupae to their dishes and keep them at 34 °C and 50% RH.
    8. Remove pupae that show melanization or leakage during the experiment.
    9. At 72 hours post-injection, collect the surviving pupae into 15 mL tubes and store at −80 °C.

  2. Virus purification

    The virus used for injections comes from infected bee material. How much preparation you need depends on the form you start with.

    Starting material

    • Whole pupae: symptomatic, virus-infected pupae are collected and used as the virus source. You prepare the extract and quantify it yourself.
    • Lysate: an extract that is already prepared and usually already quantified by the lab that sends it.

    If you start with whole pupae

    1. Homogenize the pupae.
    2. Centrifuge at 10,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C to clarify.
    3. Filter the supernatant.
    4. Quantify the virus by qPCR (copies/µL), using the virus load calculation in step 8.
    5. Dilute to the injection dose and filter again.

    If you start with a lysate

    1. Use the concentration from the sending lab, or confirm it by qPCR.
    2. Dilute to the injection dose and filter.
    Filtering after dilution: a second filtration after diluting is recommended. It is not needed if you dilute with high-quality, cell-culture-grade PBS and are confident in your sterile technique.

  3. RNA extraction with TRIzol

    1. Grab a cold tube rack from the −20 °C freezer and put labelled 1.5 mL tubes for each sample in it, along with small metal beads (number of beads depends on type/size of sample).
    2. Add 1 mL of TRIzol to each sample tube.
    3. Put samples in the homogenizer with the following setting: 30 seconds, 1.5 mL tube, 4 m/s speed.
    4. Remove from homogenizer and let samples sit at room temperature for 10 mins.
    5. Add 200 µL of chloroform to each sample.
    6. Shake samples vigorously for 10–30 seconds. Sample should be cloudy.
    7. Sit samples at room temperature for 3 mins. Final result should separate into 3 layers.
    8. Run samples in centrifuge at the following setting: 12,000 rcf, 15 mins.
      1. Whilst centrifuge is running, label new set of 1.5 mL tubes and pipette 500 µL of isopropanol into each. Place rack of samples into freezer after.
      2. Set up a rack inside fume cupboard to hold 2 falcon tubes for waste, one for TRIzol and one for alcohol.
    9. Take tubes carefully out of the centrifuge and use P200 to take off the top layer and put into new test tube. Aim for 400–600 µL of sample. Do not touch other layers.
    10. Leave tubes in ice for 15 mins (or leave overnight if not enough time to complete protocol).
      1. While waiting, grab the magnet and a beaker to remove the beads and wash them.
    11. Put samples in centrifuge at 12,000 rcf for 10 mins.
      1. While waiting, grab a lid from a mini test tube rack and lay 4–6 Kimwipes on the bottom of the tray.
    12. Carefully remove tubes from centrifuge and pour off liquid into TRIzol waste falcon tube. Place sample tubes upside down on Kimwipes so no supernatant left.
    13. Slowly pipette 1000 µL 80% ethanol into each sample tube. Try not to disturb pellet too much.
    14. Centrifuge samples at 7,500 rcf for 5 mins. Replace Kimwipes in tray.
    15. Gently remove tubes and pour off supernatant carefully into ethanol waste falcon tube. Do not disturb pellet.
    16. Repeat steps 13–15. Leave tubes upright at room temperature for at least 15 mins to air-dry (can leave for another 5 mins if liquid is still present). Remaining drops can be pipetted out with P10.
    17. Place samples in cold tube rack from −20 °C freezer and pipette 100 µL of elution buffer into each. Pipette in and out multiple times to ensure good distribution. Further vortexing can be done too until complete dissolution.

    You will need (8 samples)

    8 mL TRIzol · 1.6 mL chloroform · 4 mL isopropanol · 16 mL 80% ethanol · 800 µL elution buffer

  5. RNA quantification and equalization

    Measure RNA concentration and purity on the NanoDrop. Good RNA usually has an A260/A280 around 2.0. Then dilute every sample to the same concentration, 50 ng/µL, so each cDNA reaction starts with the same amount of RNA (10 µL × 50 ng/µL = 500 ng).

    RNA stock volume = (target concentration × final volume) ÷ stock concentration

  6. cDNA synthesis (High Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit)

    20 µL reaction: 10 µL master mix + 10 µL RNA (equalized to 50 ng/µL).

    Master mix +12% extra

    Steps

    1. Place reagents on ice and mix gently. Do not vortex.
    2. Distribute 10 µL master mix to tubes, strips or plates.
    3. Add 10 µL RNA template to each and mix by pipetting.
    4. Add 10 µL water to the non-template control.
    5. Keep on ice until loading into the thermal cycler.

  8. Virus load calculation

    Method: absolute quantification with a standard curve. Each virus has its own standard curve (a dilution series of known copy numbers), which gives a slope and an intercept. These convert a sample’s Cq into copies.

    Formulas

    log₁₀ copies = (Cq − intercept) ÷ slope

    copies/µL = 10log₁₀ copies (rounded; set to 0 if Cq > 32, treated as not detected)

    Final value for graphs and statistics = log₁₀(copies + 1)

    The “+1” keeps samples with 0 copies in the analysis, because log₁₀(0) does not exist; log₁₀(0 + 1) = 0.

    Example values are the DWV-B standard curve. Type Cq values, one per line (sample name optional).

  9. Immune gene expression

    Method: relative quantification with the 2−ΔCq method, normalized to the geometric mean of the housekeeping (reference) genes. The number of housekeeping genes can differ between studies; use the mean of all the ones you ran.

    Formulas

    Reference Cq = geometric mean of the housekeeping gene Cqs = (Cq₁ × Cq₂ × … × Cqₙ)1/n

    (n = number of housekeeping genes)

    ΔCq = Cqgene − reference Cq

    Relative expression = 2−ΔCq

    Unlike virus loads, this gives expression relative to the reference genes, not copy numbers. A smaller ΔCq means higher expression.

    Type the Cq of each immune gene for this sample, one per line (gene name optional).
