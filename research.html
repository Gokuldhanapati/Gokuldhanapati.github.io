Research

My PhD research focuses on understanding the relationship between honey bee health, viral infections, and environmental stressors that contribute to colony decline. In particular, I study how viruses interact with their honey bee hosts and how these interactions influence colony survival over time.

The overall goal is to identify key biological and environmental factors associated with colony loss, with a focus on viral dynamics, immune responses, and seasonal stress conditions.

Research experience and responsibilities:

  • Field sampling and honey bee colony management, including beekeeping practices and routine monitoring of colony health and seasonal dynamics.
  • Design and execution of honey bee injection-based bioassays to study virus–host interactions under controlled experimental conditions.
  • Molecular and virological analyses including virus purification, RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and quantitative PCR (qPCR) for virus detection and gene expression profiling.
  • Support for supervisor-led extension and outreach activities focused on honey bee health management and applied apiculture.
  • Collaboration with multiple laboratories and contribution to interdisciplinary research projects across different experimental systems.
