Research

PhD Research

Jan 2025 – Present

My PhD research focuses on understanding the relationship between honey bee health, viral infections, and environmental stressors that contribute to colony decline. In particular, I study how viruses interact with their honey bee hosts and how these interactions influence colony survival over time.

The overall goal is to identify key biological and environmental factors associated with colony loss, with a focus on viral dynamics and immune responses.

Research experience and responsibilities:

  • Honey bee colony management and field sampling of adult bees and eggs.
  • Design and execution of honey bee injection-based bioassays for virus–host interaction studies.
  • Virus purification, RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and qPCR-based molecular analyses.
  • Support for honey bee health extension and outreach activities.
  • Collaboration with multiple laboratories to conduct research.

Publications: In preparation

2020 – 2022

My research objectives included studying the biodiversity of coccinellids, analyzing the genitalia structures and polymorphism of common coccinellids, and investigating the seasonality of Micraspis spp. in rice-based cropping systems.
